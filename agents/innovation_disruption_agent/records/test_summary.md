## Creative destruction

- Bank intake: could create convenience and advisor capacity while reducing routine elicitation/structuring work. Gains and whole-role displacement are unproven.
- Fintech: could enable scalable digital service and displace routine human recommendation activities; whether this changes the existing business model is unresolved. Vendor fees, exceptions, and commoditization may absorb value.
- Meeting summaries: could create employee time and consistent draft documentation while reducing manual note drafting. Review/correction costs may offset gains.

These are conditional analytical mechanisms, not empirical findings. Early-adoption and delayed-response exposures are considered without assuming competitor behavior.

## Ambiguous case and revision evidence

The fintech case exposed an analytical usability weakness: a conditional Low-to-High span was transparent but too broad to guide management and could be misread as a rating. It was not a structural validation failure.

Revision 0.2 adds a baseline sufficiency check, explicit withholding when decisive baseline facts are missing, separate conditional alternatives, and up to three prioritized discriminating questions. Supported single or adjacent-level provisional judgments remain allowed.
